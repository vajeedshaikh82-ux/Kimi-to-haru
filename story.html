The Story

A Year Worth Remembering

Follow Ren and Hina through four seasons in Hoshikawa City.

Premise

Where Everyday Moments Become Cherished Memories

Ren Aizawa has spent two years of high school the same quiet way — mornings on the train, afternoons in the music room, evenings on the rooftop with a guitar he rarely lets anyone hear. He's not lonely, exactly. He's just never had a reason to be loud.

Then Hina Tachibana transfers into his class on the first morning the cherry blossoms bloom, camera already in hand, and asks him a question no one else has bothered to: "What does Hoshikawa look like to you?"

What starts as a favor — helping a near-stranger photograph a year of small, ordinary miracles — slowly becomes something neither of them expected: a friendship that teaches Ren to speak in more than melodies, and teaches Hina that the best photographs are the ones you almost didn't take.

Seasons

The Shape of a Year

  • Spring — First PetalHina arrives in Hoshikawa. A camera, a quiet boy, and the beginning of a year-long project.
  • Early Summer — The Sound After RainA storm strands them together. Ren plays for someone other than himself for the first time.
  • Summer — Summer Left BehindA festival, a confession half-spoken, and a friend group that starts to feel like something more permanent.
  • Autumn — Borrowed TimeThe photo project nears its final season, and everyone begins to wonder what happens after graduation.
  • Winter — Quiet StreetsDistance and doubt creep in, testing what the year has built.
  • New Spring — Where Spring WaitsThe cherry trees bloom again — and this time, Ren is the one asking the question.
Themes

What This Story Is Really About

Friendship First

A slow, believable romance built on years of small kindnesses rather than grand gestures.

The Beauty of the Ordinary

Train platforms, rainy afternoons, and rooftop conversations — the story finds meaning in what's easy to overlook.

Music as Language

For a boy who struggles to say what he feels, a guitar says it for him.
